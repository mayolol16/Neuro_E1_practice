### Q109  
*Source: VR Booster — Lecture slides 6-8 + Content Q4: CN III infarct*

A 66-year-old woman with hypertension and type 2 diabetes awakens with double vision. She cannot open her left eye without lifting the lid with her finger. Her eyes are shown with the lid held open. The left pupil is 3 mm and briskly reactive. Which is the most likely cause?

![Figure](images/eye_l_cn3_infarct.svg)

- **A.** Lesion of the left abducens nerve
- **B.** Interruption of the left sympathetic chain
- **C.** Lesion of the left trochlear nerve
- **D.** Infarct of the left oculomotor nerve
- **E.** Compression of left CN III by uncal herniation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

The left eye rests down and out (unopposed superior oblique and lateral rectus) with complete ptosis (levator palpebrae), but the pupil is normal. Per the content table, CN III INFARCT (stroke / microvascular disease) causes eye down and out, blocked medial gaze, and ptosis with a NORMAL pupil. Compression (PComm aneurysm, uncal herniation) causes the opposite pattern: mydriasis and loss of the light reflex with normal eye position early on.

> **High-yield:** CN III infarct: down & out + ptosis, pupil spared. CN III compression: blown pupil, eye position normal.

</details>

### Q110  
*Source: VR Booster — Lecture slide 7: Testing CN III muscles*

During an extraocular movement exam, a student wants to isolate the action of the left inferior oblique muscle. Which maneuver best tests this muscle?

- **A.** Ask the patient to look straight up from primary gaze
- **B.** Adduct the left eye, then ask the patient to look up
- **C.** Abduct the left eye, then ask the patient to look up
- **D.** Abduct the left eye, then ask the patient to look down
- **E.** Adduct the left eye, then ask the patient to look down

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Per the lecture: medial rectus = adduct; superior rectus = ABduct then elevate; inferior rectus = ABduct then depress; inferior oblique = ADDuct then elevate. The obliques are tested with the eye adducted (content Q7: move the eye medially, then look up and down).

> **High-yield:** Recti tested in ABduction, obliques tested in ADDuction. IO = adduct + up; SO = adduct + down.

</details>

### Q111

### Q112

- **B.** Left abducens nerve
- **C.** Left oculomotor nerve
- **D.** Right oculomotor nerve
- **E.** Left trochlear nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: E**

Loss of the superior oblique (CN IV) impairs turning the affected eye down and medially. Patients tilt their head AWAY from the affected side, which compensates for the extorted, elevated eye. A head tilt toward the right shoulder therefore points to a LEFT CN IV lesion.

### Q118  
*Source: VR Booster — Case 5: Cavernous sinus thrombosis – pupils*

A man with septic thrombosis of the LEFT cavernous sinus has a left CN III palsy; the right CN III is normal. A penlight is shone into his RIGHT eye. What is the expected response?

- **A.** Neither pupil constricts
- **B.** Right pupil constricts; left pupil is sluggish
- **C.** Right pupil is sluggish; left constricts normally
- **D.** Left pupil constricts; right pupil is sluggish
- **E.** Both pupils constrict normally

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Light in the right eye travels via the intact right optic nerve to both pretectal areas and both Edinger-Westphal nuclei. The right CN III is intact, so the right pupil constricts (direct). > **High-yield:** Efferent (CN III) lesion: the affected pupil fails no matter which eye is stimulated.

</details>

### Q121

### Q123

### Q130  
*Source: VR Booster — Content Q5: Posterior commissure*

- **B.** Normal direct and consensual responses
- **C.** Loss of the consensual response only
- **D.** Loss of the direct response only
- **E.** Loss of both direct and consensual responses

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Each eye projects to BOTH pretectal nuclei (via crossed and uncrossed fibers at the chiasm), and each pretectal nucleus projects to both Edinger-Westphal nuclei. With a left FEF infarct, the intact right FEF dominates and pushes the eyes LEFT (toward the lesion), with paralysis of right gaze. Rule: in FEF stroke the eyes look TOWARD the lesion.

> **High-yield:** FEF stroke: eyes look TOWARD the lesion. FEF seizure: eyes look AWAY.

</details>

### Q141  
*Source: VR Booster — Content Q10: RT FEF vs LT PPRF*

A patient's eyes rest in the position shown. A lesion of the left PPRF could explain this. A lesion at which other single site would produce the SAME resting eye position?

![Figure](images/eye_both_right.svg)

- **A.** Right PPRF
- **B.** Right abducens nerve
- **C.** Left frontal eye field
- **D.** Right frontal eye field
- **E.** Left medial longitudinal fasciculus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Both eyes are deviated to the patient's RIGHT. A left PPRF lesion lets the right PPRF dominate, turning the eyes right (paralysis of left gaze). A right FEF lesion removes drive to the left PPRF, so the left FEF dominates and the eyes also turn right. The content answer key: RT FEF lesion and LT PPRF lesion both position the eyes to the RIGHT.

> **High-yield:** RT FEF lesion = LT PPRF lesion (eyes right). FEF lesion → look toward lesion; PPRF lesion → look away.

</details>

### Q142  
*Source: VR Booster — Lecture slide 44: FEF seizure*

A 19-year-old woman has a focal seizure originating in the LEFT frontal eye field. During the seizure, which eye movement is expected?

- **A.** Eyes remain midline with nystagmus
- **B.** Both eyes deviate upward
- **C.** Both eyes deviate to the right
- **D.** Both eyes deviate to the left
- **E.** Only the left eye deviates to the right

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q143

### Q144

### Q146  
*Source: VR Booster — Lecture slides 39-40 + Content Q10: Vertical gaze*

A 67-year-old man has an infarct of the dorsal (posterior) midbrain. Which eye-movement deficit is most likely?

- **A.** Absent pupillary light reflex in one eye only
- **B.** Paralysis of conjugate gaze to the right
- **C.** Unilateral down-and-out eye with ptosis
- **D.** Inability to adduct one eye on lateral gaze
- **E.** Paralysis of conjugate vertical gaze

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: E**

The vertical gaze center is in the midbrain (superior colliculus/pretectal area), and its axons cross in the posterior commissure. ### Q148  
*Source: VR Booster — Content Q12: Types of eye movements*

A birdwatcher keeps a flying hawk centered in his vision as it glides slowly across the sky. Which type of eye movement is he using?

- **B.** Vestibulo-ocular reflex
- **C.** Smooth pursuit
- **D.** Vergence
- **E.** Saccade

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

Smooth pursuit tracks a moving object. Saccades jump between stationary objects; vergence moves the eyes in different directions (convergence/divergence); the VOR moves the eyes opposite to head movement to stay on a stationary target.

> **High-yield:** Pursuit = moving target; saccade = jump between stationary targets.

### Q149

### Q150  
*Source: VR Booster — Content Q12: Conjugate vs vergence*

Which eye movement involves the two eyes moving in DIFFERENT directions?

- **A.** Saccade
- **B.** Vergence
- **C.** Horizontal gaze
- **D.** Smooth pursuit
- **E.** Conjugate gaze

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Conjugate movements move both eyes in the same direction (saccades, pursuit, horizontal and vertical gaze). Vergence moves the eyes in different directions, e.g., convergence of both medial recti during the near response.
