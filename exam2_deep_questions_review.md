### Question 1 (Original Q64) — Auditory & Vestibular System

**Stem:** A 38-year-old woman presents with episodic vertigo, tinnitus, and hearing loss in the right ear. The episodes last for hours. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A)** Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo 
- **B)** Inner ear suppurative labyrinthitis 
- **C)** Acute post-viral vestibular neuritis 
- **D)** Recurrent episodic Meniere disease **(Correct)**

**Explanation:** Meniere's disease is characterized by the triad of episodic vertigo (lasting minutes to hours), tinnitus, and hearing loss (often with a feeling of ear fullness). It is caused by endolymphatic hydrops. Attacks are recurrent and can eventually lead to permanent hearing loss.

*High-Yield Rule: Recurrent episodic Meniere disease*

### Question 3 (Original Q110) — Basal Ganglia

**Stem:** In Parkinson disease, which of the following best describes the effect of dopamine loss on the basal ganglia circuitry?

- **A)** Increased activity of the direct pathway and decreased activity of the indirect pathway 
- **B)** Decreased activity of the direct pathway and increased activity of the indirect pathway **(Correct)**
- **C)** Increased activity of both direct and indirect pathways 
- **D)** Decreased activity of both direct and indirect pathways 

**Explanation:** Dopamine normally excites the direct pathway (via D1 receptors) and inhibits the indirect pathway (via D2 receptors). Both effects facilitate movement. In Parkinson disease, loss of dopamine leads to decreased activity of the direct pathway and increased activity of the indirect pathway, resulting in reduced thalamic excitation and bradykinesia.

*High-Yield Rule: Decreased activity of the direct pathway and increased activity of the indirect pathway*

### Question 6 (Original Q62) — Auditory & Vestibular System

**Stem:** A 70-year-old man presents with hearing loss. The Weber test lateralizes to the left ear. The Rinne test shows air conduction greater than bone conduction in both ears. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A)** Left conductive hearing loss 
- **B)** Right sensorineural hearing loss **(Correct)**
- **C)** Left sensorineural hearing loss 
- **D)** Right conductive hearing loss 

**Explanation:** In sensorineural hearing loss, the Weber test lateralizes to the better (good) ear. If the Weber lateralizes to the left, the left ear is better (or the right ear is worse). Since both Rinne tests are normal (AC > BC), the conductive mechanism is intact. Therefore, this patient has right sensorineural hearing loss.

*High-Yield Rule: Right sensorineural hearing loss*

### Question 13 (Original Q1) — Visual System

The right PPRF coordinates rightward eye movements; when lesioned, the unopposed left PPRF drives the eyes to the left. A stroke is a common cause.

*High-Yield Rule: Right paramedian pontine reticular formation*

### Question 146 (Original Q58) — Auditory & Vestibular System

**Stem:** A 45-year-old woman presents with a sensation of the room spinning when she rolls over in bed. The episodes last about 30 seconds. She has no hearing loss or tinnitus. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A)** Recurrent Meniere disease 
- **B)** Benign positional vertigo **(Correct)**
- **C)** Post-viral vestibular neuritis 
- **D)** Inflammatory labyrinthitis 

**Explanation:** BPPV is characterized by brief episodes of vertigo (typically less than 1 minute) triggered by changes in head position (rolling over, looking up). It is caused by otoconia dislodging from the utricle and entering the semicircular canals. The Dix-Hallpike maneuver is diagnostic. Treatment is the Epley maneuver.

*High-Yield Rule: Benign positional vertigo*

### Question 147 (Original Q63) — Auditory & Vestibular System

### Question 148 (Original Q71) — UMN / LMN

**Stem:** A 58-year-old man presents with deviation of the tongue to the right on protrusion. He also has right-sided arm and leg weakness. Which of the following is the most likely location of his lesion?

- **A)** Right hypoglossal nerve 
- **B)** Right hypoglossal nucleus 
- **C)** Left corticobulbar tract **(Correct)**
- **D)** Left hypoglossal nerve 

**Explanation:** The tongue deviates toward the side of weakness. This patient has right-sided tongue weakness, which could be due to a right LMN lesion (hypoglossal nerve or nucleus) or a left UMN lesion (corticobulbar tract). The associated right-sided arm and leg weakness indicates a left-sided UMN lesion (above the pyramidal decussation). Therefore, the tongue deviation to the right is due to a left corticobulbar lesion.

*High-Yield Rule: Left corticobulbar tract*

### Question 149 (Original Q7) — Visual System

**Stem:** A 24-year-old woman presents with vision loss in her right eye only. She reports that when she closes her left eye, she cannot see anything out of her right eye. Which structure is most likely lesioned?

- **A)** Right optic tract 
- **B)** Optic chiasm 
- **C)** Right optic nerve **(Correct)**
- **D)** Right lateral geniculate nucleus 

**Explanation:** Monocular vision loss indicates a lesion anterior to the optic chiasm, affecting the optic nerve of that eye. The optic nerve carries all visual information from one eye, so a complete lesion produces total blindness in that eye (unilateral anopia). Lesions at the chiasm or more posteriorly affect both eyes (producing hemianopias or quadrantanopias).

*High-Yield Rule: Right optic nerve*

### Question 150 (Original Q29) — Visual Reflexes

**Stem:** A 65-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after a fall. He is comatose. On examination, his left pupil is dilated and unreactive to light, and his left eye is deviated down and out. Which of the following is the most likely cause of his coma and eye findings?

- **A)** Left temporal uncal herniation **(Correct)**
- **B)** Right temporal uncal herniation 
- **C)** Left medial thalamic hemorrhage 
- **D)** Bilateral frontal lobe contusion 

**Explanation:** In uncal (transtentorial) herniation, the medial temporal lobe uncus herniates across the tentorial notch and directly compresses the ipsilateral (left) oculomotor nerve (CN III). This manifests as an ipsilateral fixed and dilated pupil with ptosis and a 'down-and-out' eye deviation, accompanied by progressive midbrain compression leading to coma.

*High-Yield Rule: Left temporal uncal herniation*
